Structural risk drivers

Allen et al. (2017) find that emerging zoonoses most often arise in tropical forest regions with high mammal diversity, changing land use and growing populations: mainly West & Central Africa, South & Southeast Asia and parts of Latin America.

Weak preparedness amplifies risk: the Global Health Security Index 2021 found that no country was fully prepared for a major epidemic or pandemic.

World Bank influenza pandemic scenarios (Burns, van der Mensbrugghe & Timmer, 2008). Model estimates, not forecasts.

Method and sources

Base rate

Cumulative probability = 1 − (1 − p)n, assuming independent years (a simplification). p ≈ 2%/yr for a COVID-scale pandemic (Marani et al. 2021) and ≈ 1%/yr for a severe, 1918-scale pandemic (Madhav et al. 2017).

Risk score (estimate)

Pathogen class × 2 (novel/unknown or respiratory = 4, henipa/hanta = 3, haemorrhagic fever or mpox = 2, vector-borne/other = 1) + log₁₀(cases + 1), capped at 3, + 2 if multi-country, + 2 if reported ≤ 14 days ago (1 if ≤ 45 days). Rescaled to 0–10. Respiratory pathogens weigh most because historical pandemics have almost always spread through the airways.

    Frequently asked questions

    What Pandemicradar is, which outbreaks it follows, and how often the figures change.

    What is Pandemicradar?

    Pandemicradar is a daily risk indicator. It combines published historical base rates for pandemics with the latest outbreak reports from WHO, ECDC and CDC. It is not a forecast of where or when the next pandemic will start.

    Which outbreaks does Pandemicradar follow?

    The site highlights signals such as H5N1 bird flu, Ebola, MERS and other coronaviruses, mpox, measles, dengue, Nipah and yellow fever when those diseases appear in WHO Disease Outbreak News, ECDC weekly reports, or CDC travel and bird-flu updates.

    Where do the data come from?

    Live inputs are the WHO Disease Outbreak News API, the ECDC Communicable Disease Threats Report feed, CDC Travel Health Notices, and the CDC A(H5) bird flu situation summary. Base rates cite Marani et al. 2021 and Madhav et al. 2017.

    How often is Pandemicradar updated?

    The dataset is refreshed every day around 06:30 in Stockholm (05:30 UTC during standard time). Each page shows the timestamp of the latest snapshot.

    Is this medical advice or a prediction?

    No. Scores are estimates from a documented formula, and the probabilities are published base rates, not a prediction. The site is not medical advice.
