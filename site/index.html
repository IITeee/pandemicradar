How likely is the next pandemic?

Historical base rates combined with the latest outbreak reports from WHO, ECDC and CDC, refreshed daily.

Not a prediction. Nobody can forecast exactly where or when the next pandemic will begin. This site is a transparent risk indicator: probabilities come from published base rates, and the risk scores are our own estimates from a simple, documented formula.

Structural risk drivers

Allen et al. (2017) find that emerging zoonoses most often arise in tropical forest regions with high mammal diversity, changing land use and growing populations: mainly West & Central Africa, South & Southeast Asia and parts of Latin America.

Weak preparedness amplifies risk: the Global Health Security Index 2021 found that no country was fully prepared for a major epidemic or pandemic.

Consequences: three scenarios

World Bank influenza pandemic scenarios (Burns, van der Mensbrugghe & Timmer, 2008). Model estimates, not forecasts.

For comparison: WHO estimates COVID-19 excess mortality at about 14.9 million in 2020–2021. Fan, Jamison & Summers (2018) estimate expected annual losses from pandemic influenza at about US$500 billion (≈0.6% of global income), including the value of lives lost.

Method & sources

Base rate

Cumulative probability = 1 − (1 − p)n, assuming independent years (a simplification). p ≈ 2%/yr for a COVID-scale pandemic (Marani et al. 2021) and ≈ 1%/yr for a severe, 1918-scale pandemic (Madhav et al. 2017).

Risk score (estimate)

Pathogen class × 2 (novel/unknown or respiratory = 4, henipa/hanta = 3, haemorrhagic fever or mpox = 2, vector-borne/other = 1) + log₁₀(cases + 1), capped at 3, + 2 if multi-country, + 2 if reported ≤ 14 days ago (1 if ≤ 45 days). Rescaled to 0–10. Respiratory pathogens weigh most because historical pandemics have almost always spread through the airways.
